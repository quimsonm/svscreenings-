**What an abdominal ultrasound actually sees**

When we do an abdominal complete ultrasound, we're scanning the organs most likely responsible for what you've been feeling — liver, gallbladder, kidneys, spleen, pancreas, and the aorta.

We're looking for things like:

- **Gallstones** — one of the most common causes of right-sided abdominal pain and that miserable discomfort after a fatty meal
- **Fatty liver disease** — increasingly common, usually no symptoms until it's already progressed
- **Kidney stones or cysts** — often the real culprit behind flank pain people chalk up to a pulled muscle
- **Enlarged spleen or liver** — can point to inflammation, infection, or something worth a closer look
- **Aortic changes** — less common, but exactly the kind of thing worth catching early

What we *can't* see on ultrasound: IBS, diverticulitis, hiatal hernias, appendicitis. Those require different imaging — CT, scope, or a clinical exam. Ultrasound isn't the right tool for every answer.

**I'd rather tell you that upfront than have you come in expecting something we can't deliver.**

But here's what I've watched happen more times than I can count: someone comes in convinced their pain is IBS. We find gallstones. Or a cyst on the kidney they didn't know was there. Or early fatty liver disease. Or a partially blocked bile duct that explained everything.

Symptoms are your body raising its hand. They rarely map neatly to a single diagnosis. An abdominal ultrasound doesn't replace a full workup — but it puts real information in the room in a way that a Google search never will.
