## 1. Medical Director Requirement

**No Nevada statute explicitly requires a "medical director" for a standalone diagnostic ultrasound facility.**

However, two doctrines create a de facto physician oversight requirement:

### NRS 630.020 — Practice of Medicine
Defines "practice of medicine" to include diagnosing, treating, correcting, preventing, or prescribing for any human disease, ailment, or condition "by any means or instrumentality." Interpreting a diagnostic ultrasound study almost certainly falls within this definition.

### Nevada Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) Doctrine
Established by three Attorney General opinions (AG No. 219 1977, AG No. 10 2002, AG No. 1 2010) — prohibits non-physician entities from owning or controlling businesses that practice medicine. Nevada's Board of Medical Examiners is reportedly active in enforcement.

**Practical implication:** A contracted reading physician who interprets studies and bears clinical responsibility is required. The title "medical director" is not mandated by statute — but the function is. How SVS is owned and how the physician relationship is documented must be structured correctly to avoid CPOM violation.

**Common solution:** Physician-owned PC (Professional Corporation) / Management Services Organization (MSO) structure. Attorney must evaluate SVS's current ownership before scaling diagnostics.

1. Surgical center for ambulatory patients
2. Freestanding birthing center
3. Independent center for emergency medical care
4. Agency to provide nursing in the home
5. Facility for intermediate care
6. Facility for skilled nursing
7. Facility for hospice care
8. Hospital
9. Psychiatric hospital
10. Facility for the treatment of irreversible renal disease
11. Rural clinic
12. Nursing pool
13. Facility for modified medical detoxification
14. Facility for refractive surgery
15. Mobile unit
16. Community triage center
17. Rural emergency hospital

**A standalone diagnostic ultrasound service does not appear on this list.** No "outpatient imaging center," "diagnostic testing facility," or "independent diagnostic testing facility" category exists in NRS 449.

**Action required:** Get a written determination from HCQC confirming no license is required. Do not assume — get it in writing.

## 3. CLIA Certification

**Not required for diagnostic ultrasound.**

Federal CLIA (42 CFR Part 493) applies to facilities examining "specimens derived from the human body." Radiological/imaging facilities performing only imaging procedures — including ultrasound, MRI, CT — are explicitly exempt by CMS policy.

Nevada NRS 652 (Medical Laboratories) defines a "medical laboratory" as facilities performing microbiological, serological, cytological, histological, chemical, hematological, or biophysical examination of tissues, secretions, or excretions. Imaging-only ultrasound does not fit this definition.

⚠️ **Important:** If SVS ever adds blood testing, cholesterol panels, or any specimen-based service alongside ultrasound, that component would trigger CLIA and Nevada lab licensing separately.

## 4. Sonographer Credential Requirements

**Nevada does NOT require state licensure for sonographers.**

- Nevada is not among the states with mandatory sonographer licensing (only NH, NM, ND, and OR require it as of 2025)
- **NAC Chapter 653** covers ionizing radiation practitioners (radiologic technologists) — explicitly does NOT cover diagnostic ultrasound (non-ionizing sound waves)
- No Nevada Administrative Code chapter specifically governs diagnostic medical sonographer credentials

**Nevada makes no statutory distinction** between "screening" and "diagnostic" ultrasound for credentialing purposes.

**Industry standard (effectively required):**
- ARDMS (RDMS designation) — Registered Diagnostic Medical Sonographer
- ARRT (RT(S) designation) — Registered Radiologic Technologist (Sonography)

SVS team already holds these credentials. If AIUM accreditation is ever sought, ARDMS/ARRT becomes formally mandatory.

## 5. Cash-Pay vs. Insurance — What Changes

### What cash-pay REMOVES:
- Federal IDTF enrollment requirements (42 CFR 410.33) — these are Medicare billing rules only
- Insurance credentialing and payer paneling
- Prior authorization requirements
- Medicare Conditions of Participation
- Supervising physician enrollment requirement (IDTF-specific)

### What cash-pay does NOT change:
- NRS 630.020 practice of medicine definition
- Nevada CPOM doctrine
- State facility licensing (if applicable)
- State tort liability standards
- Clinical and documentation standards for diagnostic studies

## 6. "Screening" vs. "Diagnostic" Language — Important

How SVS frames and documents services has regulatory implications:

- **Physician-signed reports with clinical findings** = unambiguously diagnostic practice of medicine = requires properly structured physician relationship
- **"Wellness screening" with disclaimer that results require physician follow-up** = more regulatory ambiguity, but CPOM enforcement against this model has not been publicly tested in Nevada
- **Marketing language** should be reviewed by attorney — "diagnostic" vs. "screening" is meaningful

Current SVS model (physician-signed reports for diagnostics) is the more defensible structure as long as the physician relationship is properly documented.

## 8. Attorney Consultation Priorities

### Priority 1 — CPOM Structure
Is SVS's ownership and physician relationship properly structured to avoid practicing medicine without a license or CPOM violation? The PC/MSO structure may be needed.

### Priority 2 — Written HCQC Determination
Ask HCQC in writing whether a standalone cash-pay diagnostic ultrasound screening service requires a facility license under NRS 449 and if so, which category. Get this in writing before scaling.

### Priority 3 — Physician Services Agreement
The contracted reading physician who signs reports needs a properly drafted professional services agreement clearly defining:
- Their role and clinical responsibilities
- Liability and indemnification
- Their relationship to SVS (independent contractor vs. employee vs. PC structure)
- Turnaround requirements and communication protocols

### Priority 4 — Marketing Language Review
Have attorney review how services are described on website, in ads, and in patient-facing documents. "Diagnostic" vs. "screening" language has regulatory implications.

### Priority 5 — Future Medicare Planning
If SVS ever seeks to bill Medicare, IDTF enrollment under 42 CFR 410.33 would trigger:
- Formal supervising physician requirement
- Technician listing requirement
- Three-site limit
- Additional performance standards
Plan for this structure now even if not billing Medicare today.

SVS appears to be operating in a regulatory space that current Nevada statutes do not explicitly govern — which likely means no facility license is required. However:

1. The CPOM doctrine requires physician oversight to be properly structured — not just contracted, but documented correctly
2. A written HCQC determination should be obtained before scaling diagnostics significantly
3. The reading physician services agreement is the most critical document to get right
4. A one-time healthcare compliance attorney consultation (~$200-400) protects the entire business before investing heavily in diagnostic marketing
